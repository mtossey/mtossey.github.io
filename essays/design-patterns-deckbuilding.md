	Design patterns are like multi-purpose, common, reusable solutions to multiple problems for developers. Deckbuilding in card games such as Hearthstone, Magic the Gathering, and Legends of Runeterra are similar to this. While deckbuilding, the player needs to first consider the type of deck they wanna play and determine what the deck is good at and how the deck can win the game. Afterwards, the player then sees the common decks in the meta and the problems they can pose for the deck they want to play. Like design patterns, there will usually be a card or set of cards that can help mitigate or in extreme cases nullify a deck’s weakness.
	Just like in coding, decks need to have synergy and compatibility. For example, one would not use bubble sort over merge sort when needing a consistent runtime over large sample sizes. Deck building is the same way, as one would not put a card that heals your life in a deck that is trying to win the game as fast as possible. With this in mind, poorly chosen patterns or cards can lead to inefficiency or failure.
	Furthermore, when it comes to optimizing a deck, it is usually not done in the first build. In playtesting, one may find that certain cards are ineffective and remove them for better cards that do their job better or just do a different job entirely. Similar to coding, through trial and error developers refactor code and modify patterns to improve readability. Overall, just like design patterns can be used to solve common problems in coding, cards in deck building work in a very similar way.
